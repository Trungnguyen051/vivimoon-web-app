# An Article's inline products come from its Reading Theme, not from the Article

The Knowledge article design (Figma A2) places a three-product strip inside the article body, which reads as though the author picked those three products. Giving `Article` a `productIds` field would say exactly that, and it is the most faithful reading of the design — but it would make `editorial` reference catalog IDs, adding `DEPENDS_ON.editorial = ['catalog']` and inverting the one reason `editorial` is its own resource key: that an Article references no Product, so editorial can migrate to a CMS without waiting for catalog. Instead the strip is composed at the page from the Article's **Reading Theme**, through a `Reading Theme → collection` table in `lib/` of the same shape as `lens-entries.ts`. `Article` stays product-free, the dependency lives in the page that renders both rather than in the resource, and the strip is still relevant to what was just read.

This concept was called `Topic` in the first draft of this ADR. It was renamed during the Support-section grill, which introduced a `Topic` of its own — the six-subject taxonomy filing FAQ Entries and Guides, shown as the support hub's tiles and the FAQ page's sidebar. Both are groupings of written content, so one word across the two would have been genuinely ambiguous at every call site.

The rename stands, but not on the grounds first recorded: that draft kept `Topic` for the Support taxonomy on the basis that it alone is shopper-visible, and that a Reading Theme "is never shown to a reader by name." That is not so. The Knowledge index labels every Reading Theme in its chip row, each card wears one as its eyebrow, and an article's breadcrumb names the Reading Theme and links back to the index filtered by it. Both taxonomies are visible and both name themselves on screen — which strengthens the case for two words rather than weakening it. What actually separates them is what they file: Reading Themes file Articles, Topics file FAQ Entries and Guides. The allocation of the word `Topic` is arbitrary once that premise falls, and it is left where the Support grill put it because it had already propagated through that section's ADRs and glossary.

## Consequences

The map is partial, and modelled as such — a union in the shape of `LensEntry`, where a Reading Theme either carries a destination or carries its absence. `care` has none: it covers solution, cases and hygiene, and the catalog sells lenses. An Article under a destination-less Reading Theme renders with neither the inline strip nor the closing CTA banner, which is the honest outcome; defaulting it to `all-lenses` would put a lens strip under an article about cleaning solution.

The products in an article are not individually chosen, so an Article cannot cite a specific lens by name in its strip the way the mock copy ("Honey Brown", "Milk Tea Latte") implies. If Vivimoon later wants per-article merchandising, this is the decision to revisit — and doing so means accepting that editorial can no longer go upstream ahead of catalog.
